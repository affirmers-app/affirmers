# Affirmers: social activity, on-chain value

## 1. The gap

Attention is the scarcest asset online, and the people who earn it, creators, builders, small communities, capture very little of what their audience feels. Fans can like, repost and reply. None of that carries weight beyond the feed, and none of it reaches the person.

Meanwhile on Solana, coins are cheap to create and quick to trade, but they are usually detached from any real person doing real work. The result is noise: tokens with names and no one behind them.

## 2. The idea

Affirmers links the two. A person with a following gets a coin that carries their name. Activity around that coin feeds a shared vault reserved for them. Anyone who rates the person can send support through X, and in return share in the upside that follows. Support travels both ways: the person is backed, and the backer is not left with only a thank-you.

The loop has four moves:

1. **Turn anyone into their own coin.** A creator, a founder, a friend with a following.
2. **Every trade fuels the vault.** Trading activity routes a portion into a vault set aside for the person the coin names.
3. **Send support through X.** The money reaches the person directly; the vault handles the reward side.
4. **Take your share.** Backers share in the upside the coin creates.

## 3. Why a vault

Paying out of a single shared vault keeps incentives legible. Backers know where the reward comes from, the person knows what is reserved for them, and nobody has to trust a promise made in a reply thread. Every movement into and out of the vault is visible on chain.

## 4. What Affirmers is not

It is not a promise of return. A coin's activity can be high or low, and rewards depend on it. It is not an endorsement: naming a person does not mean they have approved a coin. And it does not hold user wallets; people sign their own transactions.

## 5. Threats and answers

| Threat | Answer |
| --- | --- |
| A coin impersonates a person | Coins are tied to a verified handle; unverified coins are labelled as such. |
| Wash trading to inflate activity | Rewards weight unique participants and age of position, not raw volume. |
| Fake support claims | Support is confirmed against the public record before any reward is released. |
| Fake site or address | The contract address appears only at the top of the official site, and only after the team states it. |

Parameters behind these answers are still being settled and are listed as open in `tokenomics.md`.

## 6. Why Solana

Low fees and quick confirmation make small acts of support economically sensible. A reward loop that costs more to join than it pays out would never work; Solana keeps the floor low enough that a small backer still matters.
